    Research · Claims, sources and limits

    Follow a sentence back to its evidence¶

    A bibliography says what was consulted. A source network says which record supports which claim, where to inspect it, how narrowly it applies, and what remains uncertain.

    This page summarizes the present network as it stands: a small, reviewed pilot for the TRS-80 Model I chapter, not yet an evidence graph for the whole book. On 26 September 2026, the structured registry contains 8 source cards and 8 bounded claim records. All eight claims concern the TRS-80 Model I and its immediate expansion or Model III continuity. The seven other machine profiles do not yet have equivalent claim-level records.

    Evidence is not a popularity contest¶

    The editorial policy uses a five-step ladder:

    Level Source character Best use
    1 Original manuals, catalogues, advertisements, software, broadcasts and period reviews What was offered, documented or said at the time—qualified for edition, market and promotional purpose.
    2 Museum, university, library and curated archival records Provenance, object identity, collection context and researched institutional interpretation.
    3 Named oral histories and participant interviews Attributed experience, motive and memory, with speaker, date, consent and context.
    4 Specialist archives and databases with inspectable provenance Technical depth, structured relationships and routes to rare evidence.
    5 Forums, social posts, auctions and unverified recollections Questions, leads and clearly labelled personal testimony—not sole support for a central fact.

    Central historical claims normally need level 1 or 2 evidence. Lower levels can be essential to discovery and lived experience, but confidence, repetition and reaction counts do not move a source up the ladder.

    The eight pilot source cards are distributed across that ladder as 4 level-1, 1 level-2 and 3 level-4 records. The level-4 records are specialist archive summaries; their individual limitations remain visible below.

    The TRS-80 pilot network¶

    The table condenses eight bounded claim records. “Context” means a source helps bound or interpret a claim but is not its sole support.

    Claim record What the bounded claim establishes Connected evidence
    claim:trs80-announcement 3 August 1977 is the release marker, while stocking and fulfilment could be staggered. Smithsonian retrospective, paras. 2–3 + 1977 catalogue, pp. 5–6 for context
    claim:trs80-us-launch-bundle The 1977 US $599.95 offer joined the keyboard computer, 12-inch monitor and CTR-41 cassette recorder. 1977 catalogue, pp. 2–4
    claim:trs80-launch-specifications The bounded launch configuration separates processor, RAM, ROM, BASIC, keyboard and display modes. 1977 catalogue, pp. 3–4 + TRS-80.org Model I specifications
    claim:trs80-level2-basic Level II was a richer BASIC in 12 KB ROM with 500-baud cassette operation. TRS-80.org Level II BASIC
    claim:trs80-interface-configuration The documented 1979 Expansion Interface position, memory capacity and connections belong to a revision-specific manual. Expansion Interface Operator's Manual, introduction, capabilities and displayed pp. 3–8
    claim:trs80-minidisk-path The 1978 disk offer had defined prerequisites and a manufacturer-claimed cassette/disk timing comparison. 1978 catalogue, catalog page 14, “TRS-80 Mini-Disk System”
    claim:trs80-retail-network Radio Shack advertised ordering and service through its US network without guaranteeing local stock. 1977 catalogue, pp. 5–6
    claim:trs80-model3-continuity The 1980 Model III integrated the physical system; “most” Model I software was a bounded compatibility promise. archived Model III description + Digital Design, October 1980, printed p. 59, “New Products: Three New TRS-80s” for context

    This network deliberately avoids claiming more than its sources. The launch bundle is limited to the 1977 US offer; dealer prices could vary. The catalogue's load-time comparison is identified as advertising, not an independent benchmark. A 1979 interface manual describes its documented revision, not every unit ever sold. The Model III's “most software” wording is preserved as a manufacturer claim rather than converted into full compatibility.

    Eight source cards, each with a job¶

    Source Recorded scope Recorded limitation
    1977 Radio Shack RSC-1 catalogue US launch offer, configuration, display and retail language Manufacturer promotion; dealer prices and local stock could vary.
    1978 Radio Shack RSC-2 catalogue, catalog page 14 US Mini-Disk offer Manufacturer promotion; timing is not an independent benchmark.
    Expansion Interface Operator's Manual Documented 1979 interface revision Revision-specific rather than universal.
    Digital Design, October 1980, printed p. 59, “New Products: Three New TRS-80s” Contemporary Model III introduction A short trade-press item; compatibility detail comes from another record.
    Smithsonian release retrospective Release date and launch context Retrospective; “goes on sale” does not prove universal same-day stock.
    TRS-80.org Model I Retrospective identity and specifications Specialist summary; launch configuration is checked against the period catalogue.
    TRS-80.org Level II BASIC ROM and cassette characteristics Specialist archive summary.
    TRS-80 Revived Model III description Archived description and compatibility notes Reproduced manufacturer material appears beside retrospective context.

    Seven of these cards are link-only for rights purposes; the Project Gutenberg manual card is recorded as public domain. Those labels govern reuse, not evidential value. Public access to a scan is never treated as permission to republish its pages or images.

    What every record must remember¶

    A durable source card records creator, title, publisher, date, source type, canonical and archived URLs where available, an exact locator, machine/market scope, supported claims, limitations, rights state, ingestion date, verifier and last check. A durable claim record identifies its subject and predicate, qualifiers, confidence, evidence relationships, page use, review date and next review.

    This structure makes several failures visible:

    • a link can break while the claim and its locator remain known;
    • two sources can disagree without one being deleted from the record;
    • a US launch bundle cannot quietly become a worldwide specification;
    • a manufacturer promise can remain attributed rather than becoming neutral fact; and
    • a claim can be revised everywhere it is used, not only on the page where an error was first reported.

    When evidence conflicts, the editor does not select the convenient number. The options are to narrow by date, market, model or revision; attribute competing accounts; lower confidence; or mark the question unresolved. Consequential claims receive at least annual review, while link health is monitored separately.

    Open evidence needs¶

    The graph's clearest limitation is breadth. It has no equivalent structured network yet for Sinclair, Commodore, Atari, BBC Micro, Apple II, TI-99/4A or Amstrad. It is concentrated on one US machine family, contains no oral-history card, and cannot yet support broad comparisons of households, education, software labour or regions.

    The next work should therefore:

    1. build atomic claim and source records for each legacy machine profile;
    2. add market and revision qualifiers before drawing cross-machine comparisons;
    3. seek institutional, period and scholarly evidence beyond manufacturer material;
    4. add oral histories only with speaker, date, context, consent and corroboration rules; and
    5. record archival and visual assets at item level, with rights and alt-text decisions before use.

    Add a missing connection¶

    If you have a stronger source, use the correction or source-lead form. Name the page and claim, link to the record, and include a page number, object identifier or timestamp. If the gap is an entire subject rather than a correction, send a content proposal. Personal recollections follow their own route and remain labelled testimony.

    A submitted link is a candidate, not an automatic citation. It is checked for identity, scope, provenance, conflicts, rights and privacy before it can enter a claim record. Publication remains a reviewed human decision.
